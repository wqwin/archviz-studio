# Interior Design Pitfall Guardrails & Rationales

When designing floor plans, CAD/SketchUp/Blender layouts, custom cabinetry, or interior lighting — **never optimize for render-only aesthetics at the expense of daily living**. Apply these guardrails proactively, and **whenever the user asks why a design choice was made, clearly explain the underlying ergonomic, physical, or maintenance rationale in plain language**.

## Communication Rule: Always Explain the "Why"
1. **Lead with direct advice** ("don't do X → do Y instead"), followed by the **concrete lived-in reason** (ergonomics, glare physics, cleaning reality, MEP clearance, or budget ROI).
2. **Tailor to real constraints**: Ask or account for household members (kids/elderly/pets), ceiling height, room dimensions, and budget before pushing trends.
3. **Hard finishes minimal, style via soft furnishings**: Never pile on complex wall moldings, full-height decorative slats, or non-functional ceilings just to "look expensive" — spend budget on irreversible layout, lighting, and storage first.

## 1. Spatial Ergonomics & Circulation Clearances

| Guardrail (What to Do / Avoid) | Why (Real-World Rationale) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dining clearance ≥ 60 cm** between table edge and walls/walkways; use round tables or banquette seating in tight dining areas instead of oversized islands. | Less than 60 cm means chairs cannot be pulled out comfortably and nobody can walk behind a seated person. |
| **Corridor clear width < 90 cm → no deep full-wall cabinets**. | Hinged doors swinging open or drawers pulling out will completely block passage and cause shoulder-bumping claustrophobia. |
| **Desk height vs. drawers**: Standard 75 cm desk should **not** have under-desk drawers; if drawers are needed, raise desk to **78–80 cm with slim drawers** (or skip drawers). | A 75 cm desk with a 10–12 cm drawer leaves too little knee/thigh clearance, forcing hunched posture and bruised knees. |
| **Kitchen golden workflow**: `Fridge (Retrieve) → Sink (Wash) → Prep Counter (Chop) → Hob (Cook) → Plating`; dishwasher immediately adjacent to sink. | Breaking this sequence forces back-and-forth "marathon" steps across the kitchen and drips water/grease across the floor when loading the dishwasher. |
| **Kitchen split-level counters (高低台)**: Main prep/sink height = `User Height ÷ 2 + 5~10 cm`; hob zone = `Main − 8~10 cm`. **Skip split-level in small kitchens.** | Raising the sink prevents lower-back strain while washing, and lowering the hob prevents raised-elbow fatigue while stir-frying. However, in small kitchens with short countertops, a step-down slices an already scarce prep surface into unusable fragments. |
| **Living room scale discipline**: Avoid oversized sectional sofas + massive coffee tables + standing AC towers in compact rooms; use a straight sofa + accent lounge chair + slim side tables. | Oversized furniture turns a living room into a cramped showroom with zero circulation and makes the room feel smaller. |
| **Bedroom clearance**: Verify nightstand width/position never blocks hinged wardrobe doors. | A classic CAD oversight that leaves the corner wardrobe door permanently half-blocked. |

## 2. Built-In Appliances & Custom Cabinetry Clearances

| Guardrail (What to Do / Avoid) | Why (Real-World Rationale) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Never place power outlets or plumbing directly behind built-in appliances** (zero-clearance fridge, steam-oven, dishwasher, washer/dryer). | Plug heads, power cords, and water hoses add 4–6 cm of rigid depth, pushing the appliance proud of the cabinetry and ruining flush alignment. Put outlets/valves in an adjacent cabinet or overhead compartment. |
| **Dishwasher depth = 60 cm *carcass* clear depth**, not countertop depth; align toe-kicks and check adjacent corner-door swing. | Countertop overhangs mask shallower cabinet carcasses; miscalculating depth or ignoring protruding handles causes the dishwasher to stick out or jam neighboring doors. |
| **Washer vs. dryer depth**: Measure both machines separately before building balcony laundry cabinets. | Heat-pump dryers are typically deeper than matching front-load washers; sizing the cabinet to the washer alone makes the dryer protrude awkwardly. |
| **Zero-clearance fridge tolerance**: Confirm wall plumbness, avoid protruding stone countertop lips, and keep side panels flush with cabinet reveals. | Crooked plaster walls or overhanging stone lips prevent the fridge from sliding in and cause door hinges to rub within a year. |
| **80% Closed / 20% Open Storage Rule**: Avoid full-wall open bookshelves or excessive glass display doors unless the client truly owns curated collections. | Empty or overstuffed open shelves collect heavy dust and instantly turn into visual clutter in everyday households. |

## 3. Lighting Physics & Surface Reflections

| Guardrail (What to Do / Avoid) | Why (Real-World Rationale) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Never pair glossy floor tiles (`亮光砖`) with trimless/no-main-light (`无主灯`) layouts**. Use matte/soft-sheen tiles (`柔光砖`) or wood flooring instead. | Glossy tiles act like mirrors, reflecting every ceiling spotlight onto the floor as harsh glare dots — looking like a supermarket aisle instead of a serene home. |
| **Anti-glare & beam placement**: Never put spotlights directly above sofa backrests or bed pillows; use deep-baffle anti-glare spotlights **~15 cm off the wall** for wall-washing, and combine them with soft ambient fill (coves/downlights). | Downward spotlights over seating or beds shine straight into eyes when relaxing; spotlights alone create harsh cave-like contrast without ambient fill. |
| **Kitchen 3-layer task lighting**: Bright main ceiling light + **under-cabinet motion/wave LED strip** + dedicated sink spotlight. | With only an overhead ceiling light, your own body casts a dark shadow directly onto the cutting board and sink; wave sensors let you toggle light with wet/oily hands. |
| **Bathroom layered lighting**: Overhead neutral/cool task light + **warm mirror-front / mirror-cabinet light**. | Overhead light alone casts unflattering under-eye shadows when grooming; mirror-front warm light illuminates the face evenly and adds depth. |
| **Kids' & elderly rooms**: Prefer high-CRI (**Ra > 90/95, RG0 low-blue-light, flicker-free**) anti-glare ceiling lights + gentle nightlights over complex spotlight grids. | Complex spotlight arrays risk glare damage to infants/children looking up and often under-illuminate spaces for elderly eyesight. |

## 4. Ceilings, Headroom & Curtain Placement

| Guardrail (What to Do / Avoid) | Why (Real-World Rationale) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Function-first ceilings**: When clear ceiling height is **< 2.6 m**, avoid full-room decorative dropped ceilings; use minimal perimeter bulkheads/double-eyelid trims (`双眼皮`) or curved beam-softening only where needed for HVAC/pipes/beams. | Unnecessary dropped ceilings steal precious headroom, create a claustrophobic feel, trap dust in deep coves, and burn budget that belongs in furniture and lighting. |
| **Open living-room + enclosed balcony**: Mount curtains on the **outer balcony window**, not across the interior living-room/balcony arch. | Hanging heavy drapes across the living-room threshold defeats the entire purpose of opening the balcony (visually shrinks the room and wastes fabric cost). |

## 5. Low-Maintenance ("去家务化") Material & Fixture Choices

| Guardrail (What to Do / Avoid) | Why (Real-World Rationale) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Basin mounting — strictly separate Kitchen vs. Bathroom**:<br>• **Kitchen sink**: Flat-rim handmade **undermount sink** (or seamless integrated stainless sink), avoid raised-rim top-mount sinks.<br>• **Bathroom vanity**: **One-piece integrated ceramic basin** + wall-mounted faucet, avoid ceramic undermount basins. | In kitchens, undermount handmade sinks let you wipe counter water straight in without hitting a top-mount lip. In damp, often dark bathrooms, the hidden stone-to-ceramic undermount seam traps standing moisture and breeds mold/grime — whereas a one-piece ceramic basin has zero seam. |
| **Kitchen countertop backsplashes**: Skip raised rear/front water-retaining strips (`挡水条`); leave a clean micro-gap sealed with epoxy grout (`美缝`). | Silicone caulk along raised strips inevitably yellows and molds within months; front lips add an extra seam that traps grease and reduces usable flat depth. |
| **Faucets & hardware**: Prefer brushed stainless/chrome over matte black or pure white faucets in hard-water areas. | Hard-water mineral spots show immediately on matte black finishes, and budget white coatings easily chip or stain. |
| **Sliding doors**: Prefer **top-hung trackless doors (`吊轨门`)** over recessed floor tracks. | Floor tracks act as permanent traps for hair, crumbs, and mop grime that are painful to clean. |
| **Shower drains**: Prefer standard square deep-water-seal floor drains over trendy **linear strip drains (`长条地漏`)**. | Linear drains catch far more hair across a wide tray, hold stagnant soapy biofilm underneath, and require prying open frequently to scrub. |
| **Cabinet & tile finishes**: Prefer matte/satin/melamine-textured doors and soft-sheen tiles over **high-gloss PET doors & glossy tiles**. | High-gloss surfaces magnify fingerprints, water streaks, and micro-scratches under raking light, making them harder to maintain and cheaper-looking over time. |
| **Limit narrow slatted feature walls (`木格栅`)**: Use selectively as an accent rather than wrapping entire rooms. | Every narrow groove collects dust and requires tedious slat-by-slat wiping. |
